How long a Queensland purchase takes to settle

Queensland contracts commonly specify 30 or 42 days from signing to settlement. The number is set in the contract rather than by law, and the finance side has to fit inside it.

Queensland, in one line

Signing a contract starts a fixed clock. Everything else, the finance approval, the valuation, the searches and the transfer, has to be finished before it runs out. That is the opposite of how buyers usually imagine it, where the loan is arranged and then a property is found. In practice the property is found first and the loan is then squeezed into a period someone else nominated.

Stage by stage, with the usual duration of each

StageWhat has to happen, and roughly how long it takes
Contract signedThe clock starts. A statutory cooling-off period applies to most residential contracts in Queensland, and its length is set by state law.
Finance applicationLodged immediately if documents are ready. Assessment commonly takes several business days, longer where income is variable or self employed.
ValuationOrdered by the lender once the file is assessed. Usually a few days to book, inspect and report, longer in regional areas where valuers travel.
Unconditional approvalIssued once the valuation and any conditions are satisfied. This is the date the contract cares about, not the earlier approval in principle.
Loan documentsIssued, signed, returned and verified. Several days, and postal or witnessing requirements can add more.
Searches and transfer dutyHandled by your conveyancer. Transfer duty is assessed and paid under the rules administered by the Queensland Revenue Office.
SettlementFunds and title exchange electronically on the nominated date.

Durations above are typical rather than guaranteed, and every one of them is a business day count rather than a calendar one. The Queensland Government's guidance on buying a home sets out the conveyancing steps that run alongside the finance ones.

What the contract date sets in motion

The finance clause names a date by which finance must be approved. That date, not the settlement date, is the one that decides whether the purchase is safe, and it is usually a fraction of the total period. Meeting it depends almost entirely on whether the application was ready to lodge on the day the contract was signed, which is why the work happens before you go looking. Understanding how capacity is worked out before you apply is what makes that possible.

Transfer duty is assessed on the transaction and is payable within a period set by the Queensland Revenue Office. Concessions exist for some buyers and they are claimed rather than applied automatically, so the entitlement is worth confirming early with your conveyancer.

The two stages that usually slip

The first is the document request. An assessor asks for something that was not supplied, the file pauses, and the days lost are days the contract does not give back. Sending a complete file at the outset removes most of this risk, and what you will be asked to hand over is knowable in advance rather than a surprise.

The second is the valuation. It cannot be ordered until the file has been assessed, it depends on a third party's availability, and in a regional market that party may be travelling to the property. When a valuation comes back below the contract price, the shortfall has to be resolved, and resolving it costs more time than the valuation itself.

What you can do to keep it on schedule

Have the finance conversation before you sign, not after. Assemble documents before they are requested. Nominate a finance date you can actually meet rather than the one suggested across a kitchen table. Appoint the conveyancer at the same time as the lender rather than a week later. And where you can, negotiate the longer of the 2 common settlement periods, because the extra days cost nothing and buy the whole schedule some room. Timeline questions

Can settlement happen faster than 30 days?
Yes, with unconditional finance, a completed valuation and both conveyancers ready. A short period leaves no room for error, so it is agreed deliberately.
What happens if finance is not approved in time?
The contract usually allows an extension by agreement. It is dealt with before the date passes, never after.
Does the settlement date move if the valuation is low?
It can. Resolving a shortfall takes time the contract may not have allowed.
